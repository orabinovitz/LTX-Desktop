# Brand Awareness Editing Playbook

## Editing Philosophy: Earn the Right to Be Watched

Brand awareness editing operates on a fundamentally different premise from performance creative. The opening frame is not the place to establish brand identity — it's the place to earn the right to be watched. Where digital ads lead with problems and solutions, brand films lead with feeling and emotion before communicating any message.

People do not buy products — they buy better versions of themselves. Opening with feature lists speaks to the rational mind; brand films must speak to the emotional mind first.

## Structure: Building Emotional Arcs in Seconds

### Three-Act Structure (25%/50%/25%)
- **30-second spot:** ~7.5s setup -> ~15s confrontation -> ~7.5s resolution
- **60-second spot:** ~15s setup -> ~30s confrontation -> ~15s resolution
- Setup: protagonist's world and emotional stakes
- Confrontation: deepens investment; brand/product emerges as solution
- Resolution: emotional payoff and brand reveal

### YouTube 0–5 Second Window (Critical)
- **0–2s:** Hook — visually surprising or emotionally resonant. NO logos, brand names, or product shots.
- **2–4s:** Natural brand introduction woven into hook context
- **4–5s:** Retention trigger — open loop, surprising claim, or teased payoff
- Ads establishing brand before the skip button see 40% higher view-through rates vs end-branding

### Pacing Principles
- Average clip length in commercials: 2–5 seconds
- Alternate tension and release even in 15-second spots
- Starting in medias res (middle of action) captures attention in short-form
- Every tempo change must serve the emotional arc — random pace changes are amateur

## Cinematic Editing Techniques

### Visual Treatments
- **Letterboxing** (black bars for widescreen look): signals premium content, differentiates from social ads
- **Film grain:** warmth and texture for nostalgic/artisanal positioning
- **Anamorphic lens looks:** distinctive oval bokeh and lens flares = high production value without dialogue

### Color Grading for Brand
- Two-step: color correction (exposure, white balance) then creative grading (mood, style)
- Use `set_color_correction` to adjust brightness, contrast, saturation, and temperature per clip
- When generating content, specify color mood in prompts (warm, cool, desaturated, high-contrast)
- **Orange-teal technique:** warm highlights + cool shadows — cinematic standard, makes skin tones pop
- Genre approaches: action = strong contrast + cool shadows; documentary = neutral palettes; luxury = desaturated + high contrast

### Sound Design
- Develop alongside visual brief from pre-production, not bolted on afterward
- Non-music audio triggers emotional response up to 1.5 seconds faster than music
- Voiceovers add 12% conversion rate increase on TikTok
- Strategic silence before key moments amplifies impact
- Consider brand-specific sound signatures — a luxury car door closing conveys solidity and cost before a word of copy

## Brand Awareness Mistakes That Waste Budgets

- **Over-branding too early.** Opening with logos, product shots, and brand colors is "clean, branded, and completely skippable." Brand signals in the first 2–3 seconds trigger ad-avoidance reflexes.
- **Assuming visibility = awareness.** Exposure without meaning is white noise. A consumer needs 5–7 interactions before they remember a brand. One campaign is never enough.
- **Unrealistic lift expectations.** Realistic brand awareness change from a single campaign is 3–5%. Expecting overnight shifts leads to premature campaign abandonment.
- **Technology without authenticity.** AI-generated imagery can backfire if audiences can't relate emotionally — craft and human connection remain non-negotiable for brand building.
- **85% of marketing campaigns flop** primarily due to fuzzy goals, bad metrics, and audience delusion.

## Platform Adaptation for Brand Awareness

### YouTube (Most Sophisticated Brand Toolkit)
- **Skippable TrueView:** CPV billing (pay when viewers watch 30s+ or click). The 5-second mark is the creative gauntlet.
- **Non-skippable 15s:** Guaranteed full delivery. Still needs a strong opening beat.
- **Bumpers (6s exactly):** Can't tell a full story but excel at reinforcing known messages. Pair with longer formats for compounding recall.
- **Shorts (70B daily views):** 9:16 vertical with native-feeling creative. Repurposed landscape = 3x lower completion.
- **Sequential strategies:** Problem -> Solution -> Urgency across 3 exposures leverages CTV's 95%+ completion rates.

### TikTok
- Communicate value proposition in first 6 seconds (90% of recall impact captured there)
- Products on screen boost brand affinity 65%, recall 25%
- 10+ unique creatives = 1.3x ad recall, 3.0x purchase intent, 1.5x awareness
- Sweet spot frequency: 2–3 exposures per week

### LinkedIn
- Under 30 seconds (200% lift in completion rates)
- Hook in first 2–3 seconds
- Sound-off design mandatory — burn in subtitles directly
- Export at highest quality available

## Brand Lift Measurement

- **Brand Lift:** Changes in consumer perception post-exposure
- **Ad Recall:** Whether viewers remember seeing/hearing a specific ad
- **Cost-Per-Lifted-User:** Total cost / lifted users (primary success metric)
- YouTube Brand Lift Study: free tool, minimum ~$15K per survey question ($30K for CTV), detect lift at ~4,100 responses per metric
- The highest VTR video is not necessarily the one driving the most brand lift — view completion and memory formation sometimes pull in opposite directions
- In 8/10 cases, ad recall-based measurement was wrong (IAB UK study of 2,000+ studies) — passive exposure measurement via pixel tagging is more reliable
