# Advertising screenwriting reference

Use this file when you need deeper craft support beyond the core skill.

## 1) Start from human truth

The script should come from an uncomfortable, recognizable, human-level truth.
Good source material:
- insecurity
- desire
- loneliness
- status anxiety
- pride
- guilt
- belonging
- self-image
- convenience people feel embarrassed to admit
- a product flaw that can be turned into honesty
- a tension the audience already lives with

Useful questions:
- What does the audience feel but rarely say out loud?
- What do they do that contradicts what they claim?
- What do they wish were true about themselves?
- What tiny daily pain or longing does this product resolve?
- What truth would make someone say: "that's exactly me"?

Bad starting points:
- feature list
- mission statement
- category cliches
- empty adjectives like innovative, premium, revolutionary, seamless

## 2) Truth + conflict

Truth alone is not enough.
Turn the truth into a problem, contradiction, pressure, or reveal.

Examples of conflict:
- You want to look composed but you are falling apart.
- You want to give something meaningful but have no idea how.
- You want control but life is messy.
- You are not yourself under stress, hunger, fatigue, or pressure.
- You want to belong, impress, protect, hide, win, escape, or prove something.

Without conflict, the script becomes description.
With conflict, the script becomes story.

## 3) Build the arc fast

Even 15 seconds can have an arc:
- hook
- tension
- turn
- payoff
- brand landing

For 30s and 60s, use:
- setup
- escalation
- reveal or reversal
- emotional/comedic landing
- brand signature

If the ending is weak, the script will usually feel forgettable even if the middle is decent.

## 4) Start from the ending

A reliable way to sharpen scripts:
- write the final image
- write the final line
- write the final emotional realization
- then build backward

Strong endings usually do one of four things:
- reveal the true meaning of what we were watching
- flip the audience's assumption
- release tension through laughter
- land a simple emotional truth cleanly

## 5) Hooks that actually stop attention

The opening should create one of:
- curiosity
- emotional unease
- a visual question
- comic surprise
- direct recognition
- tension that needs resolution

Good hook qualities:
- immediate
- visual
- specific
- dissonant with category expectations
- easy to understand, hard to ignore

Weak hooks:
- brand chest-thumping
- generic beauty shots
- exposition
- explaining the campaign before it starts

## 6) Show, don't claim

Prefer dramatization over assertion.

Weak:
- "This car is precise."
Strong:
- a scene where impossible control is demonstrated

Weak:
- "This gift really matters."
Strong:
- a story where someone quietly prepares to give it

Weak:
- "This product saves time."
Strong:
- a scene that makes wasted time viscerally painful

A useful test:
Can the audience feel the product value before anyone says it out loud?

## 7) Write in scenes, not messaging points

Think:
- action
- behavior
- environment
- sound
- contrast
- reaction
- reveal

Not:
- key point 1
- key point 2
- key point 3

Strong scripts make the argument through lived moments.

## 8) Dialogue rules

Dialogue should:
- feel spoken
- reveal character
- carry rhythm
- avoid selling-language stiffness
- never sound like a deck was converted into speech

Use:
- contractions
- fragments
- interruption
- asymmetry
- short lines
- occasional silence

Brand or product language should usually be saved for the end unless the style is intentionally direct-response or comedic.

## 9) Voiceover rules

Use VO when it adds one of these:
- inner meaning
- poetic framing
- irony against the visuals
- compression of multiple scenes
- thematic unity

Avoid VO that merely describes the picture.

A VO-heavy piece still needs visual logic.
A beautiful VO track cannot rescue a weak concept.

## 10) Silence and sound

Silence can create:
- suspense
- dignity
- intimacy
- anticipation
- discomfort

Sound design can carry story:
- repetition
- contrast
- mechanical rhythm
- breathing
- room tone
- song payoff
- withheld impact

When relevant, script sound intentionally, not as an afterthought.

## 11) Comedy craft

Comedy works best when:
- it grows naturally from a true human behavior
- the escalation follows one clear absurd logic
- the performance is committed
- the joke serves brand memory, not just entertainment

Useful comedy tools:
- escalation
- understatement
- reversal
- status shifts
- impossible confidence
- literalization of a metaphor
- deadpan delivery
- visual contradiction

Do not let the joke float free from the brand.

## 12) Pathos craft

Emotion works when:
- it is observed, not forced
- the behavior is specific
- the payoff is earned through setup
- the script trusts the audience

Avoid:
- telling viewers what to feel
- swelling empty language
- cheap sentiment
- "beautiful" scenes with no dramatic reason

Better:
- small actions
- withheld explanation
- a final reveal that recontextualizes earlier behavior

## 13) Brand landing strategies

Common ways to land the brand:
- the product resolves the exact tension of the story
- the brand frames the emotional truth
- the reveal connects directly to brand purpose
- the product appears late, but as the obvious missing piece
- the tagline names the feeling already earned by the film

Weak landings:
- tacked-on logo with unrelated slogan
- abrupt feature explainer after an emotional story
- legalese and claims killing the mood
- over-branding early and often without narrative justification

## 14) Structural templates

### A. Problem / Resolution
Best for:
- clear practical pain points
- product-led categories
Flow:
- painful truth
- escalation
- product intervention
- relief
- brand line

### B. Without / With
Best for:
- showing absence sharply
- dramatizing everyday reliance
Flow:
- life without
- friction intensifies
- with-product turn
- transformed ease
- brand line

### C. Setup / Reveal
Best for:
- emotional storytelling
- holiday, family, gifting, identity
Flow:
- mysterious behavior
- audience assumption
- reveal of true motivation
- emotional reframe
- brand line

### D. Vignette / Anthem
Best for:
- broad audience statements
- cultural or identity-driven brands
Flow:
- linked moments
- recurring pattern
- accumulating meaning
- simple thematic landing
- brand line

### E. Experiment / Observation
Best for:
- social truth
- documentary style
- earned authenticity
Flow:
- frame a prompt or experiment
- capture reactions
- reveal pattern
- thematic conclusion
- brand line

### F. Comic Escalation
Best for:
- social spots
- memorable character ads
Flow:
- recognizable truth
- ridiculous extension
- bigger extension
- hardest laugh
- brand line

## 15) Length guidance

### 6s bumper
One image. One turn. One line.
No setup-heavy concepts.

### 15s
Usually one beat plus one reveal.
Very little dialogue.
Needs immediate clarity.

### 30s
Best all-around unit for:
- setup
- escalation
- payoff
- brand line

### 60s
Allows:
- character investment
- mystery
- emotional reveal
- richer pacing
But still requires ruthless economy.

## 16) What makes a script feel expensive vs cheap

Expensive-feeling:
- one strong idea
- disciplined restraint
- confidence in what to omit
- clear visual identity
- fewer, better lines
- a memorable ending

Cheap-feeling:
- too many messages
- generic "cinematic" language
- random inspiration phrases
- copy that explains what the film should already show
- trying to sound important instead of being clear

## 17) Diagnosis checklist for weak scripts

If a script feels weak, identify the dominant failure:
- no real insight
- no tension
- weak opening
- visually generic
- over-written dialogue
- unclear emotional shift
- disconnected brand landing
- too many ideas
- too safe
- too derivative of known ad tropes

Fix the root problem, not just the wording.

## 18) Revision moves that usually improve a draft

- make the truth more specific
- remove one full message
- start later
- end later or earlier, whichever lands harder
- replace a line with a visual
- add a turn
- sharpen the contrast
- cut anything explanatory
- make the final line simpler
- make the brand role more inevitable
